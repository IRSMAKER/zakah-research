# The Quranic Meaning of Z-K-W (زكاة) — Comprehensive Research Document

**Prepared from:** verse-by-verse study of all **56 distinct Z-K-W root verses** (59 occurrences,
20 word forms) in `quran-simple-min.txt` (Tanzil Simple-Minimal, 6236 verses), with
diacritics-stripped matching via `quran-clean.txt`.

**Method:** Each verse studied in its Quranic context (surrounding verses + thematic parallels),
with grammar, lexicon, and cross-referencing of every parallel formula in the Quran. Look-alike
forms were excluded after reading them: **زكريا / وزكريا** (Zechariah — root ز-ك-ر, 7 refs) and
**يلمزك** (9:58 — "finds fault with you"; the ك is a pronoun suffix, not a radical). Study order:
thematic clusters A→E (root anchors → the claim and the grant → the purifier sent → the founding
formula → the institution), canonical order inside each cluster.

**Files:** `1_Zakah_Verses.md` (collected verses), `2_Study_Order.md` (study plan, all 56 ticked),
`3_Study_Notes.md` (verse-by-verse journal), `PROGRESS.md` (live thesis draft).

## Part 1 — The settled core

The root Z-K-W (ز-ك-و) names **growth-through-purity**: the release of a portion that cleans what
remains and causes it to increase. Not a tax, not a percentage, not a once-a-year receipt — a
transfer whose stated double effect is **cleansing and growth**.

The Quran supplies every term of the definition in its own words:

1. **Purity is stated.** 9:103: `خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا` —
   "Take from their wealth a ṣadaqah by which you **cleanse** them and **grow-purify** them." The
   two verbs share one object and one instrument. `ṭahāra` removes; `zakāh` increases.
2. **Growth is stated.** 30:39 contrasts ribā — which "does not grow with God" — with zakāh, whose
   givers are named **الْمُضْعِفُونَ** (*al-muḍʿifūn*, the multipliers). 2:276 corroborates: God
   erases ribā and **makes the charities grow** (`yurbī al-ṣadaqāt`).
3. **The instrument is stated.** 92:18: `الَّذِي يُؤْتِي مَالَهُ يَتَزَكَّىٰ` — "who **gives his
   wealth**, purifying himself." Giving wealth *is* self-purification. The verb's object is the
   self; the verb's instrument is money.
4. **The act is stated.** 23:4: `وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِلزَّكَاةِ فَاعِلُونَ` — "those who are **doers**
   of the zakāh." A tax is paid; a zakāh is *done*.
5. **The agent is stated.** 4:49, 24:21, 53:32: no one purifies himself; God purifies whom He
   wills. The human act is the receptor of a divine grant, never a self-salvation.

The root has **three stable poles**:

| Pole | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **STATE** | purity-growth as a quality of a self, soul, child, or food | 18:74 · 19:19 · 19:13 · 18:81 · 18:19 · 91:9 |
| **ACT** | the giving that produces it — done, given, sent ahead | 9:103 · 92:18 · 23:4 · 30:39 · 2:110 |
| **GRANT** | God and His Messenger as the agents who actually purify | 4:49 · 24:21 · 2:129 · 3:164 · 62:2 |

The **STATE** pole is what the word *is*. The **ACT** pole is how it is *done*. The **GRANT** pole
is *who makes it work*. The same stem, three directions.

The root also has a **law of increase** (Part 9) and a **breach-pattern** (Part 11): in every
withholding-context, the withholder has first *sold* something — revelation for a small price
(2:174, 3:77), the Hereafter for the present (41:7), or his own verdict of purity (4:49, 53:32).
The anti-zakāh is always a **sale**, and zakāh is always a **release**.

## Part 2 — Cluster A: The Root Anchors (17 verses)

The root's intrinsic meaning, read before any institution is attached: purity as a quality of a
living self, a child, food, a gaze, a boundary.

**A1 (18:74)** — "أَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًا زَكِيَّةً بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ" — a **pure soul** killed without a
matching soul. Purity is guiltlessness of a life. The root's first appearance is a person, not a
purse.

**A2 (19:19)** — "غُلَامًا زَكِيًّا" — a **pure boy**, announced to Maryam. Purity is given before
the person acts. Surah 19's purity-thread: Yahya receives *zakāh* (19:13), Isa is announced *zakī*
(19:19), Maryam is purified (3:42).

**A3 (19:13)** — "وَحَنَانًا مِّن لَّدُنَّا وَزَكَاةً" — **a zakāh** given to Yahya beside tenderness.
Here the noun is an **inner endowment**, not money. The institutional zakāh is one visible form of
this endowment, not a different word.

**A4 (18:81)** — "خَيْرًا مِّنْهُ زَكَاةً" — better **in zakāh**. The word is a **measurable grade**:
one child can be better in zakāh than another. Purity can increase.

**A5 (18:19)** — "أَيُّهَا أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًا" — which food is **purest**. The root measures quality:
clean, wholesome, unspoiled. The contrast is corruption entering a thing.

**A6 (2:232)** — "ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ" — **more purifying and cleaner**. The
Qur'an's own gloss-pair. `ṭahāra` removes defilement; `zakāh` adds soundness. The pair recurs at
9:103 and 33:33.

**A7 (24:28)** — "هُوَ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ" — returning from a house that did not admit you is **purer**.
A boundary kept is purity.

**A8 (24:30)** — "ذَٰلِكَ أَزْكَىٰ لَهُمْ" — the lowered gaze and guarded chastity are **purer**. The
first wealth to be purified is the eye and the member.

**A9 (91:9)** — "قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن زَكَّاهَا" — **flourished who purifies it** (the soul). THE
CENTRE. The object of tazkiyah is the self; the root's success-word is bound to it; its opposite is
"buried it" (91:10).

**A10 (79:18)** — "هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ" — "will you purify yourself?" addressed to
**Pharaoh**. The invitation is universal, not sectarian.

**A11 (87:14)** — "قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ ۞ وَذَكَرَ اسْمَ رَبِّهِ فَصَلَّىٰ" — purified, then
mentioned the Name, then **stood**. THE BRIDGE TO Ṣ-L-W: the two roots meet in one verse-pair.

**A12 (20:76)** — "وَذَٰلِكَ جَزَاءُ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ" — **the reward of the one who purified**. Tazkiyah
is the entry-condition to Eden.

**A13 (35:18)** — "وَمَن تَزَكَّىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَتَزَكَّىٰ لِنَفْسِهِ" — purification is **only for
oneself**. Non-transferable, self-benefiting. The same verse pairs it with establishing the standing.

**A14 (92:18)** — "الَّذِي يُؤْتِي مَالَهُ يَتَزَكَّىٰ" — **gives his wealth, purifying himself**. THE
EQUATION: the verb's object is the self, its instrument is wealth. The sequel names the intention:
"only seeking the face of his Lord Most High" (92:20).

**A15 (80:3)** — "لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّىٰ" — perhaps the **blind man** would purify. Purity is not
purchased by rank.

**A16 (80:7)** — "وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ" — purification **cannot be coerced**. The
Messenger conveys; the person acts.

**A17 (53:32)** — "فَلَا تُزَكُّوا أَنفُسَكُمْ" — **do not declare yourselves pure**. The verdict is
forbidden, not the act. The anti-self-certification rule.

## Part 3 — Cluster B: The Claim and the Grant (4 verses)

Who may purify, and who is refused.

**B1 (4:49)** — "يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم ۚ بَلِ اللَّهُ يُزَكِّي مَن يَشَاءُ" — **claim vs grant**. The
same verb, two subjects: human self-purification is a boast; divine purification is the reality.

**B2 (24:21)** — "مَا زَكَىٰ مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ أَبَدًا وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يُزَكِّي مَن يَشَاءُ" — even
the root's simplest verb, *to be pure*, is impossible without God's favour and mercy. THE GRANT.
This verse forbids reading zakāh as self-salvation.

**B3 (2:174)** — "وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمْ" — the concealers of the Book, who sell it for a small price,
are **not purified**. Withholding purification is the punishment. Note the parallel to 4:10:
concealing revelation is eating fire.

**B4 (3:77)** — "وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمْ" — the four-fold negation: no share, no speech, no look, no
purification, for those who **sell** God's covenant for a small price. The anti-zakāh is a sale;
zakāh is a release.

## Part 4 — Cluster C: The Purifier Sent (4 verses)

The Messenger as the agent of tazkiyah.

**C1 (2:129)** — Abraham's prayer: recite, teach the Book, teach wisdom, **purify**. Tazkiyah is the
goal of revelation and the Messenger's commission — a formation, not a collection.

**C2 (2:151)** — "يُزَكِّيكُمْ" — purification is mediated by a human **from among you**. Nearness is
part of the mechanism.

**C3 (3:164)** — "مَنَّ اللَّهُ" — purification is a **favour**, not a wage. Order: recite, purify,
teach — one is purified in order to learn.

**C4 (62:2)** — "وَإِن كَانُوا مِن قَبْلُ لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُّبِينٍ" — tazkiyah is an **exodus from clear
error**. The same transfer-structure as Ṣ-L-W's "out of darkness into light" (33:43).

## Part 5 — Cluster D: The Founding Formula (14 verses)

Covenant, prophets, and the ṣalāh-zakāh pair.

**D1 (98:5)** — THE MINIMAL RELIGION: sincere worship + uprightness + establish the standing + give
the zakāh = "the religion of the upright." Zakāh is one of four constitutive acts, not an optional
extra.

**D2 (2:43)** — the pair commanded to a whole community, with a shared posture: "bow with those who
bow."

**D3 (2:83)** — zakāh inside a social-ethical covenant; the breach is `tawallā` — the same
turning-away as the Ṣ-L-W fire-verses.

**D4 (5:12)** — condition #2 of a five-condition covenant; the fifth is "lend God a good loan." The
economic axis runs through the covenant, and the return is transferred to the hereafter.

**D5 (2:110)** — zakāh is **sent ahead**; whatever good you send forward you find with God. An
investment, not a loss.

**D6 (2:177)** — zakāh appears **after** the list of spontaneous givings. Two layers: the open hand
of love, then the standing obligation of the community. The giver gave "despite love of it."

**D7 (2:277)** — zakāh is the **anti-ribā act**: no fear, no grief, immediately after "God makes the
charities grow" (2:276).

**D8 (7:156)** — zakāh is a condition for **mercy being written**. The giver is inside the circle.

**D9 (19:31)** — Jesus: enjoined on me the standing and the zakāh **as long as I live**, tied to
`barakah` — blessedness, increase. No state, treasury, or quota in the verse.

**D10 (19:55)** — Ishmael commanded his **household** the pair. The family is the first unit of the
institution.

**D11 (21:73)** — "We **inspired** them … the giving of the zakāh." The giving is revelation-shaped
and a mark of leadership. Leaders are defined by what they give, not collect.

**D12 (22:78)** — zakāh belongs to the **eased religion**: no constriction. Establish, give, hold
fast.

**D13 (4:162)** — the givers are a distinct class alongside the **firmly-rooted in knowledge**. Zakāh
is an act of the knowing.

**D14 (5:55)** — zakāh is the **mark of God's party**; the giver is an ally of God. "While bowing"
fuses the two acts.

## Part 6 — Cluster E: The Institution (17 verses)

Community, power, economy, and the increase.

**E1 (9:5)** — the **peace-condition**: repent, establish, give → release their way.

**E2 (9:11)** — the **brotherhood-condition**: the same three → brothers in religion. Withholding
keeps one outside the bond.

**E3 (9:18)** — a condition for **maintaining God's houses**: believe, establish, give, fear only
God.

**E4 (9:71)** — the **economic nerve** of the believing community, men and women alike.

**E5 (9:103)** — THE MECHANISM: take **from** their wealth a ṣadaqah that cleanses and grows them.
One act, two effects; the partitive `min` is decisive.

**E6 (22:41)** — the **first act of the empowered**: establish, give, enjoin, forbid. The test of
power is whether it gives.

**E7 (23:4)** — THE ACT: "**doers** of the zakāh." A tax is paid; a zakāh is done. It sits among
character-items, not line-items.

**E8 (24:37)** — the **economic test**: neither trade nor sale distracts from remembrance,
establishing, and giving.

**E9 (24:56)** — a condition for **mercy**, paired with obedience to the Messenger.

**E10 (27:3)** — the act of the **Hereafter-certain**. The one sure of the return gives now.

**E11 (30:39)** — THE PIVOT: ribā does not grow with God; zakāh does, and its givers are
**al-muḍʿifūn, the multipliers**. The Qur'an moves the increase-verb from the lender to the giver.

**E12 (31:4)** — word-for-word identical to 27:3: the formula is **stable across suras**, therefore
an institution, not a local instruction.

**E13 (33:33)** — the household command, ending in **ṭahāra**: remove impurity, purify. Zakāh and
purification are one movement.

**E14 (41:7)** — THE NEGATIVE DEFINITION: withholding zakāh is coordinated with **disbelief in the
Hereafter**. The giver believes in the return; the withholder denies it.

**E15 (58:13)** — zakāh follows **forgiveness**. The failed giver is re-commanded; grace precedes
the act.

**E16 (73:20)** — the **eased arrangement**: commanded in the same breath as the concession. "Lend
God a good loan" makes the giver a creditor of God.

**E17 (4:77)** — the **peace-act and courage-training**: giving loosens the grip on wealth before
life is asked for.

## Part 7 — The Noun-Lexicon of Z-K-W

| Lexeme | Form | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZAKĀH | noun (f.) | the growth-purity; the institutional giving that produces it | 2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 98:5, etc. (26×) |
| ZAKĀH | indefinite noun | a purity-endowment; a grade of purity | 19:13 · 18:81 |
| ZAKĀH | indefinite noun | any act of zakāh-giving | 30:39 |
| ZAKĪ | adjective | pure, guiltless, sound | 18:74 · 19:19 |
| AZKĀ | elative | purer, more wholesome, better | 18:19 · 2:232 · 24:28 · 24:30 |
| MUZAKKĪ / TAZKIYAH | Form II verb / verbal noun | the act and process of purifying-growing | 2:129, 3:164, 9:103, 91:9 |
| MUTAZAKKĪ | Form V participle | the self-purifier | 92:18 · 35:18 · 87:14 |

The same noun serves the **quality** (19:13, 18:81), the **act** (30:39), and the **institution**
(2:43 and the pair-formula). The word did not begin as a tax and acquire a spiritual gloss; it began
as a quality of a sound self and became the name of the act that produces that quality.

| Voice | Form | Where | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple state | زَكَا | 24:21 | to be pure (negated for humans without grace) |

| Form II active (human claim) | يُزَكُّونَ | 4:49 | to declare oneself pure |
| Form II imperative/negated | تُزَكُّوا | 53:32 | do not declare yourselves pure |
| Form II perfect | زَكَّاهَا | 91:9 | he purified it (the soul) |
| Form V | تَزَكَّىٰ / يَتَزَكَّىٰ | 87:14 · 20:76 · 35:18 · 92:18 · 79:18 | to purify oneself / grow in purity |

The voice-coverage is complete: **state** (zakā), **act** (zakkā, tazakkā), **grade** (azkā),
**quality** (zakī), **institution** (zakāh). Every voice names the same field: purity that grows.

## Part 9 — The Increase-Law

| Verse | What increases | What does not |
|---|---|---|
| 30:39 | zakāh given seeking God's face → **al-muḍʿifūn** (the multipliers) | ribā given to increase in people's wealth → **does not grow with God** |
| 2:276 | God **makes the charities grow** (`yurbī al-ṣadaqāt`) | God **erases ribā** (`yamḥaqu`) |
| 2:277 | the givers: reward, **no fear, no grief** | the accumulator: anxiety |
| 92:18-21 | the giver who seeks God's face: **will be satisfied** | the one who gives to be repaid: no reward |
| 9:103 | the taken ṣadaqah **cleanses and grows** | — |
| 73:20 | what you send ahead you **find with God**, better and greater | what is kept is not sent |

The Qur'an's economic theology is a single transfer: **the increase-verb is moved from the lender to
the giver.** Ribā is the attempt to make money grow by charging; zakāh is the release by which God
makes it grow. The root's name for the second is *zakāh* — growth.

## Part 10 — The Two-Effect Vocabulary (ṭahāra + zakāh)

| Verse | Cleansing | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| 9:103 | `tuṭahhiruhum` — cleanses them | `tuzakkīhim` — grows-purifies them |
| 2:232 | `aṭhar` — cleaner | `azkā` — more purifying |
| 33:33 | `yuṭahhirakum` — purify you thoroughly / remove the impurity | the zakāh commanded in the same verse |
| 2:174 | — | `lā yuzakkīhim` — no purification (the negative) |
| 24:21 | — | `mā zakā … abadan` — never pure (the negative) |

`Ṭahāra` is subtraction: remove what defiles. `Zakāh` is addition: cause what remains to grow. The
Qur'an consistently puts them in that order. A reading that makes zakāh only "charity" loses the
second half; a reading that makes it only "growth" loses the first.

## Part 11 — The Breach-Pattern (Sale → Loss)

| Verse | What was sold | What is lost |
|---|---|---|
| 2:174 | concealed the Book, sold it for a small price | God will not purify them; fire in the belly |
| 3:77 | sold God's covenant and oaths for a small price | no share, no speech, no look, no purification |
| 41:7 | withheld the zakāh | reckoned a disbeliever in the Hereafter |
| 4:49 | claimed self-purity | the claim is corrected by the divine grant |
| 53:32 | declared the self pure | the verdict belongs to God alone |
| 24:21 | followed Satan's steps | no one would ever be pure without grace |
| 2:83 | turned away from the covenant | the giving abandoned with the standing |

The anti-zakāh is always a **sale**: something that should be released is converted into a price.
The withholder does not merely keep money; he sells a covenant, a revelation, or a verdict. Zakāh is
the reverse: it releases the portion without a price.

## Part 12 — The Wealth-Table (what is released, to whom)

| Verse | The released thing | The recipients / shape |
|---|---|---|
| 2:177 | wealth, despite love of it | relatives, orphans, the poor, the wayfarer, askers, slaves |
| 9:103 | **from** their wealth, a ṣadaqah | taken by the Messenger; cleanses and grows |
| 92:18 | his wealth | given not to repay a favour, seeking God's face |
| 30:39 | any zakāh | given seeking God's face; multiplied |
| 2:3 / 8:3 | what God provided | spent (the open pair with the standing) |
| 2:110 / 73:20 | whatever good is sent ahead | found with God |
| 5:12 | a good loan to God | repaid with covering and gardens |
| 2:274-276 | charity, secret and public | God makes it grow |

The object is always **a portion**, never the whole; the direction is always **outward**, never
upward to a collector for its own sake; the return is always **with God**, never in the amount given.

## Part 13 — The Self-Table (the nafs as object)

| Verse | The self-word | The act |
|---|---|---|
| 91:9-10 | `al-nafs` (91:7) | purified / buried |
| 53:32 | `anfusakum` | do not declare pure |
| 4:49 | `anfusahum` | they declare pure; God purifies |
| 35:18 | `nafsihi` | purifies only for himself |
| 92:18 | implied self | gives wealth, purifying himself |
| 24:21 | `minkum` | not one of you would be pure |
| 18:74 | `nafsan zakiyyah` | a pure soul |
| 79:18 | `tazakkā` | will you purify yourself? |

The root's constant object is the **self**. Money is the instrument, never the subject. This is the
single strongest argument against reading zakāh as a revenue tax: the grammar's object is always a
person, and the wealth is always the means.

## Part 14 — The Time-Table

| Time-form | Where | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Lifelong | 19:31 (`mā dumtu ḥayyā`) | the command runs as long as one lives |
| Habitual / household | 19:55 (`kāna yaʾmuru ahlahu`) | transmitted daily in the family |
| Standing practice | 27:3 · 31:4 (`yuʾtūna`) | a participle, not an event |
| Sent ahead | 2:110 · 73:20 (`tuqaddimū`) | value moved into the future |
| Before the Day | 2:254 · 14:31 (parallel) | giving belongs to this side |
| After forgiveness | 58:13 | grace precedes the act |
| In ease and constraint | 73:20 · 22:78 | calibrated to capacity |

Zakāh is a **standing practice** (participle) with a **lifelong horizon** and a **daily household
form**. It is not a annual event in the Qur'an's grammar; the annual cycle is a later juristic
specification, and no percentage is given in the text.

## Part 15 — The Covenant-Table

| Verse | The covenant | Zakāh's place |
|---|---|---|
| 2:83 | Israel: one God + social excellence + speech + standing + giving | inside the list; breach = turning away |
| 5:12 | Israel: five conditions | condition #2, with the good loan as #5 |
| 7:156 | mercy written for the guarding, giving, believing | condition of mercy |
| 98:5 | the upright religion | one of four constitutive acts |
| 22:78 | the milla of Abraham, no constriction | establish, give, hold fast |
| 9:11 | the brotherhood covenant | the third condition |
| 9:5 | the peace covenant | the third condition |

Zakāh is never a standalone rule. It is always a **clause in a covenant** — with God, with the
community, or with the household. To withhold it is to break a relationship, not to fail a
transaction.

## Part 16 — The Face-Table (the intention)

| Verse | The intention-clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 30:39 | `تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ` | seeking God's face — the condition of increase |
| 92:20 | `إِلَّا ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ الْأَعْلَىٰ` | only seeking the face of his Lord Most High |
| 2:177 | `عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ` | despite love of it — the inner cost |
| 9:103 | taken by the Messenger, for their purification | the act has a minister, not a market |
| 2:110 / 73:20 | `لِأَنفُسِكُم` | for yourselves — the self-benefit |
| 4:49 / 53:32 | the self as claimant | the wrong intention: self-certification |

The intention decides whether the release is zakāh or a sale. The same coin can be ribā, a price, or
a zakāh; the difference is the face sought.

## Part 17 — The Bridge Verses

**Surah 87 (the hinge to Ṣ-L-W):** 87:14 "flourished who purified" → 87:15 "and mentioned the Name
of his Lord and stood." `tazakkā` → `dhakara` → `fa-ṣallā`. The two roots of this project meet in
one verse-pair: purification prepares the standing; the standing carries the purified. And 87:18-19
says this was already written in the scrolls of Abraham and Musa.

**Surah 92 (the bridge to the noun):** 92:5-7 the giver, guarder, truster → 92:18 "who gives his
wealth, purifying himself" → 92:19-21 "not to repay a favour, only seeking the face of his Lord
Most High; and he will be satisfied." The verb `tazakkā` is defined by the act of giving wealth.
This is the exact point where the quality becomes the institution.

**Surah 9 (the mechanism):** 9:103 "take **from** their wealth a ṣadaqah that cleanses and grows
them" → 9:99 spending counted as the Messenger's ṣalawāt → 9:84 the withheld ṣalāh on the dead. The
giving, the standing, and the honor-economy are one system.

**Surah 30 (the pivot):** 30:39 ribā does not grow; zakāh multiplies. The same sura opens with the
Romans' defeat and closes with the economics of the face. Increase belongs to God, not to the
lender.

**Surah 2 (the two layers):** 2:177 the spontaneous givings, then the zakāh; 2:275-277 ribā erased,
charity grown, the givers fearless. The chapter's economic argument runs from the open hand to the
standing obligation to the promise of no grief.

## Part 18 — The Settled Meaning

The root Z-K-W (ز-ك-و) names **growth-through-purity**: the release of a portion that cleans what
remains and causes it to increase — a sustained practice, not a percentage, not a receipt.

**The SAME stem, THREE directions:**

- **STATE — what the word is.** A quality of a sound self: a pure soul (18:74), a pure boy (19:19),
  a purity-endowment (19:13), a grade of betterment (18:81), wholesome food (18:19), a kept boundary
  (24:28), a guarded gaze (24:30). The root's success-word belongs here: "flourished who purifies
  it" (91:9).

- **ACT — how it is done.** The release of a portion of wealth: taken from their wealth (9:103),
  given despite love of it (2:177), given not to be repaid (92:18), given seeking the Face (30:39),
  done as a work (23:4), sent ahead (2:110), lent to God (5:12, 73:20). The act has two effects —
  cleansing and growth — and one intention — the Face.

- **GRANT — who makes it work.** God purifies whom He wills (4:49, 24:21); the Messenger is sent to
  purify (2:129, 2:151, 3:164, 62:2); no one purifies himself (24:21); no one may declare himself
  pure (53:32). The human act is the receptor of a divine grant, never a self-salvation.

**The noun zakāh** is the institutional name of the ACT pole, willingly kept and publicly
regulated — one of the four constitutive acts of the upright religion (98:5), a clause in every
covenant, the mark of God's party (5:55), the first act of the empowered (22:41), the economic nerve
of the community (9:71), and the condition of brotherhood (9:11) and mercy (24:56).

**The verb zakkā** names the act and its effect: to purify and to cause to grow. **The elative
azkā** names the grade: one thing can be purer than another. **The adjective zakī** names the
quality: sound, guiltless, clean.

**The law of increase** is the root's own economics: ribā claims increase and has none with God;
zakāh is the release God multiplies, and its givers are named *the multipliers* (30:39). The Qur'an
moves the increase-verb from the lender to the giver.

The word is therefore neither "alms" nor "tax" nor "purification" alone. It is **the growth that
comes by release** — the portion let go so that the self, the wealth, and the community may be
clean and increase. The Quran builds its covenant, its community, its economy, and its hope of Eden
on this single motion: **give the portion, and grow.**

## Conclusion

The Qur'an uses the root Z-K-W (ز-ك-و) to name **growth-through-purity** — the release of a portion
that cleanses what remains and causes it to increase. The same stem names:

- **The pure self** (STATE pole): the sound soul, the pure child, the wholesome food, the kept
  boundary, the guarded gaze — a quality that can be graded and given.
- **The giving** (ACT pole): the portion taken from wealth, given despite love of it, given without
  return, given seeking the Face — a work with doers, sent ahead, lent to God.
- **The Purifier** (GRANT pole): God who purifies whom He wills, and the Messenger sent to recite,
  teach, and purify — so that no one claims the verdict for himself.

The root's own law is the law of increase: what is released grows; what is grasped does not. Ribā
is the attempt to grow by holding; zakāh is the growth that comes by letting go. The Quran's word
for the second is the word for purity itself.

The zakāh is both what you **are** (a self being made sound) and what you **do** (a portion released
into the hands of God). Its measure is not a percentage but a direction: **outward, toward the Face,
for the growth of what remains.**
