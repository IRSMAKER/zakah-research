# Zakah — In Plain Language

A short account of what the study found, without the technical apparatus.

---

## The word in one sentence

**Zakah is the growth that comes by release:** you give away a portion of what you have, and that
giving both cleans what remains and makes it increase.

---

## What the Quran actually says

### 1. It names two effects, not one

> "Take from their wealth a charity by which you **cleanse** them and **grow-purify** them." (9:103)

Most people hear only the first half — giving as charity, giving as duty. The verse names a second
effect: growth. The Arabic uses two verbs for one act. Cleansing is subtraction; zakāh is addition.

### 2. The Quran says what grows

> "Whatever you give as ribā so that it may increase in people's wealth — it does not increase with
> God. And whatever you give of zakāh, seeking God's face — those are the **multipliers**." (30:39)

This is the key verse. The Quran takes the word "increase" away from interest and gives it to zakāh.
Interest claims to make money grow; the Quran says it does not grow with God. Zakāh is the giving God
multiplies. The people who do it are literally called "the multipliers."

### 3. Giving wealth is how you purify yourself

> "Who gives his wealth, purifying himself." (92:18)

This is the definition. The verb is about the self; the instrument is money. The Quran does not say
"pray to become pure" here — it says *give*, and you are purified.

### 4. It is something you DO, not something you pay

> "Those who are **doers** of the zakāh." (23:4)

Everywhere else the Quran says "give the zakāh." Here it calls zakāh a work with doers. A tax is
paid; a zakāh is done. And the Quran never gives a percentage. It gives a direction.

### 5. You cannot purify yourself

> "Do not declare yourselves pure." (53:32)
> "Not one of you would ever be pure without God's favour and mercy." (24:21)

This is the safeguard. The act is yours, but the result is God's. Zakāh is not a spiritual
achievement you can boast about. It is a channel for a gift.

## The three sides of the word

| Side | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A quality** | a sound, pure, growing self | a pure soul (18:74), a pure child (19:19), wholesome food (18:19) |
| **An act** | giving a portion of wealth | take from their wealth (9:103), give despite love of it (2:177) |
| **A gift** | God and the Messenger make it work | God purifies whom He wills (4:49); the Messenger purifies (62:2) |

The same word covers all three. That is why "charity" is too small a translation: charity is only
the middle column.

---

## Why it is always next to the prayer

Zakāh appears with the prayer in most of its verses. The Quran treats them as two halves of one
life:

- The prayer is the vertical act — turning toward God.
- The zakāh is the horizontal act — turning toward people with what you have.

And one verse puts them in order:

> "Flourished who purified, and mentioned the Name of his Lord, and stood." (87:14-15)

Purify, remember, stand. The giving prepares the standing.

---

## What withholding it means

The Quran is unusually sharp here:

> "Those who do not give the zakāh, and they are disbelievers in the Hereafter." (41:7)

Withholding is not treated as stinginess. It is treated as unbelief in the return — the belief that
nothing comes back, so nothing should be let go. The person who gives believes there is a return.
The person who withholds has decided there is not.

And the contrast is always a **sale**:

- They sold the Book for a small price — so God will not purify them (2:174, 3:77).
- They kept what should have been released — so they are counted deniers of the Hereafter (41:7).

Zakāh is the opposite of a sale. It is a release with no price.

---

## What this means in practice

1. **It is a direction, not a percentage.** The Quran commands the release and names its intention;
   it does not fix a rate. A rate can be a useful discipline, but it is not the meaning.
2. **The intention is the face of God.** The same coin can be interest, a price, or a zakāh. The
   difference is what you are seeking (30:39, 92:20).
3. **It is for the growth of what remains.** You are not losing the portion; you are removing what
   would have corrupted the rest.
4. **It belongs to the community.** It is a condition of brotherhood (9:11), of peace (9:5), of
   maintaining God's houses (9:18), and of the first act of anyone given power (22:41).
5. **It is done, not paid.** Do it as a work — planned, habitual, quiet, and without a claim.

---

## The sentence to remember

**Give the portion, and grow.**

That is the root: purity by release, increase by letting go.
