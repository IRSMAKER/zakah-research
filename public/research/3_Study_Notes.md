# CLUSTER A — ROOT-LANGUAGE ANCHORS
Purity and growth outside the money-form. These verses define what the root means before any
institution is attached to it.

## A1 — 18:74 (نفسًا زكية)
**Text:** … أَقَتَلْتَ نَفْسًا زَكِيَّةً بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ …
**Grammar:** `nafsan` object + `zakiyyatan` adjective; `bi-ghayri nafs` = without a matching soul
(no life-for-life claim).
**Context:** Musa objects to the killing of a boy who had done no wrong.
**Finding:** The root's first appearance in this cluster names a **pure living self**. `zakiyyah`
is a quality of a person, not a quantity of money. Purity here is *guiltlessness* — a life not yet
stained. The word's base sense is therefore moral/biological soundness, and the monetary zakāh will
have to be derived from that, not the reverse.

## A2 — 19:19 (غلامًا زكيًّا)
**Text:** قَالَ إِنَّمَا أَنَا رَسُولُ رَبِّكِ لِأَهَبَ لَكِ غُلَامًا زَكِيًّا
**Grammar:** `ghulāman` object + `zakiyyā` adjective, indefinite — a pure boy, quality ascribed at
birth.
**Context:** The angel's announcement to Maryam.
**Finding:** Purity is **given before the person acts**. The child is `zakī` by grant, not by
achievement. Surah 19 runs a purity-thread: Yahya receives `zakāh` (19:13), Isa is announced
`zakī` (19:19), and Maryam is chosen and purified (3:42). The root's first mode is *endowment*.

## A3 — 19:13 (وزكاة)
**Text:** وَحَنَانًا مِّن لَّدُنَّا وَزَكَاةً وَكَانَ تَقِيًّا
**Grammar:** `zakātan` indefinite accusative, coordinated with `ḥanānan` — two granted qualities.
**Context:** The gifts given to Yahya: judgment as a child, tenderness from Us, a zakāh, and he was
guarded.
**Finding:** **The noun zakāh appears here as a non-monetary endowment.** It sits in a list of inner
gifts, not in a list of payments. The word can therefore name an interior state of purity-growth.
The later institutional zakāh is one visible form of this endowment — not a different word.

## A4 — 18:81 (خيرًا منه زكاة)
**Text:** فَأَرَدْنَا أَن يُبْدِلَهُما رَبُّهُمَا خَيْرًا مِّنْهُ زَكَاةً وَأَقْرَبَ رُحْمًا
**Grammar:** `khayran` + `minhu` + `zakātan` (specification / tamyīz): better *in respect of zakāh*.
**Context:** The killed boy is replaced by a better child.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **measurable grade**: one child can be *better in zakāh* than another. It is
not a fixed tax but a scale of soundness and growth. And the verse is the direct sequel to 18:74:
the first boy was `zakī`, the replacement is *better in zakāh* — purity can increase.

## A5 — 18:19 (أزكى طعامًا)
**Text:** فَلْيَنظُرْ أَيُّهَا أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًا فَلْيَأْتِكُم بِرِزْقٍ مِّنْهُ
**Grammar:** `azkā` elative + `ṭaʿāman` specification: which is purest/best in food.
**Context:** The sleepers send a coin to the city to find the purest food.
**Finding:** The root measures **quality** — clean, wholesome, unspoiled, lawful. Food is `zakī`
when nothing corrupt has entered it. The contrast-cluster is *corruption entering a thing*; zakāh
is the state of a thing with nothing corrupt in it.

## A6 — 2:232 (أزكى لكم وأطهر)
**Text:** ذَٰلِكُمْ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ وَأَطْهَرُ
**Grammar:** two elatives: `azkā` and `aṭhar`.
**Context:** Do not prevent divorced women from remarrying their husbands by mutual consent.
**Finding:** **The Qur'an's own gloss-pair: zakāh + ṭahāra.** They are companions, not synonyms:
`ṭahāra` removes what defiles; `zakāh` adds soundness and growth. The same pair returns in 9:103
(`tuṭahhiruhum wa tuzakkīhim`) and 33:33 (`yudhhiba ʿankumu-l-rijs… wa yuṭahhirakum`). This is the
decisive lexical control: **cleanse first, then grow**.

## A7 — 24:28 (هو أزكى لكم)
**Text:** وَإِن قِيلَ لَكُمُ ارْجِعُوا فَارْجِعُوا هُوَ أَزْكَىٰ لَكُمْ
**Grammar:** elative, predicate of the act of returning.
**Context:** Permission before entering houses; if refused, return.
**Finding:** A **boundary kept is purity**. Zakāh is not only about money: it is the whole economy of
not taking what is not yours. The root's field includes the restraint of the hand and the foot.

## A8 — 24:30 (ذلك أزكى لهم)
**Text:** قُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّوا مِنْ أَبْصَارِهِمْ وَيَحْفَظُوا فُرُوجَهُمْ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ أَزْكَىٰ لَهُمْ
**Grammar:** elative predicate of the double restraint.
**Context:** Lowered gaze and guarded chastity.
**Finding:** The first "wealth" to be purified is the **eye and the member**. Before the purse, the
root names the body's own spending. Tazkiyah begins in what a person lets in, not only in what he
gives out.

## A9 — 91:9 (من زكاها)
**Text:** قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن زَكَّاهَا
**Grammar:** `man` + Form II `zakkā` + 3fs suffix `-hā` → the *nafs* (91:7-8).
**Context:** The soul and its proportions, inspired with its wrong and its guard.
**Finding:** **THE CENTRE.** The object of tazkiyah is the SELF. The root's success-word (`aflaḥa`)
is bound to it, and its opposite is `dassāhā` — buried, smothered, earthed over (91:10). Purity is
a *work on the soul*, and flourishing is defined by it. Everything monetary in the root must be
read as a means to this verse.

## A10 — 79:18 (هل لك إلى أن تزكى)
**Text:** فَقُلْ هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ
**Grammar:** Form V `tazakkā` — self-purification undertaken; interrogative invitation.
**Context:** Musa is commanded to speak to Pharaoh.
**Finding:** Purification is offered to a **tyrant**, not reserved for the pious. The invitation is
universal. Form V makes the person the agent of his own purification — but B2 (24:21) will show that
even this agency is impossible without grace. The two truths are held together, not resolved.

## A11 — 87:14 (من تزكى)
**Text:** قَدْ أَفْلَحَ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ ۞ وَذَكَرَ اسْمَ رَبِّهِ فَصَلَّىٰ
**Grammar:** `tazakkā` then `dhakara` then `fa-ṣallā` — three verbs, one sequence.
**Context:** The success-formula of Surah 87, declared already written in the scrolls of Abraham and
Musa (87:18-19).
**Finding:** **THE BRIDGE TO Ṣ-L-W.** Purification, then Name-mention, then standing. The two roots
of this project meet in one verse-pair: `tazakkā` (Z-K-W) and `fa-ṣallā` (Ṣ-L-W). Zakāh is the
*preparation* of the standing; the standing is the *act* that follows purification. They are not two
separate pillars but one movement with two verbs.

## A12 — 20:76 (جزاء من تزكى)
**Text:** جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ … وَذَٰلِكَ جَزَاءُ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ
**Grammar:** `jazāʾu man tazakkā` — the reward is defined by the act.
**Context:** The success of those who come to God with a sound heart (20:75-76).
**Finding:** Tazkiyah is the **entry-condition to Eden**. The reward is attached to the act of
purification itself, not to a payment. The verse names the destination of the root's imperative.

## A13 — 35:18 (يتزكى لنفسه)
**Text:** وَمَن تَزَكَّىٰ فَإِنَّمَا يَتَزَكَّىٰ لِنَفْسِهِ ۚ وَإِلَى اللَّهِ الْمَصِيرُ
**Grammar:** `fa-innamā` — restriction: purifies *only* for himself.
**Context:** No soul bears another's burden; warn those who guard the unseen and establish the
standing.
**Finding:** Tazkiyah is **non-transferable and self-benefiting**. No one can purify on another's
behalf; no one can outsource it. And the same verse pairs it with `aqāmū al-ṣalāh` — the ṣalāh-zakāh
pair appears even here, in a verse about individual responsibility.

## A14 — 92:18 (يؤتي ماله يتزكى) — THE BRIDGE
**Text:** الَّذِي يُؤْتِي مَالَهُ يَتَزَكَّىٰ
**Grammar:** relative clause: `yuʾtī mālahu` (gives his wealth) + `yatazakkā` (purifying himself) —
the second verb states the PURPOSE/EFFECT of the first.
**Context:** Surah 92: the giver, the guarder, the truster (92:5-7); then the one who gives his wealth
*not* to repay a favour, only seeking the face of his Lord Most High (92:19-21).
**Finding:** **THE EQUATION.** The Qur'an defines self-purification by *giving wealth*. The verb's
object is the self; the verb's instrument is money. This is the exact junction where "purity"
becomes "the giving," and it is the verse from which the noun `zakāh` takes its institutional
meaning. Note the intention-clause: `illā ibtighāʾa wajhi rabbihi-l-aʿlā` — the same `wajh Allāh` as
30:39.

## A15 — 80:3 (لعله يزكى)
**Text:** وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّىٰ
**Grammar:** Form V, assimilated `yazzakkā`; `laʿalla` = perhaps.
**Context:** The blind man interrupts the Prophet, who frowns and turns away.
**Finding:** The **poor and blind** may be the one who purifies. Purification is not purchased by
social rank, and the Messenger is rebuked for preferring the powerful. The root's field is opened to
the least.

## A16 — 80:7 (ألا يزكى)
**Text:** وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّىٰ
**Grammar:** negative `allā` + Form V.
**Context:** The same passage: the wealthy man who does not purify.
**Finding:** Purification **cannot be coerced**. The Messenger's duty is conveyance; the act belongs
to the person. This protects the word from becoming a tax enforced by power — it is an invitation
before it is an obligation.

## A17 — 53:32 (فلا تزكوا أنفسكم)
**Text:** فَلَا تُزَكُّوا أَنفُسَكُمْ ۖ هُوَ أَعْلَمُ بِمَنِ اتَّقَىٰ
**Grammar:** prohibitive `lā` + Form II jussive, plural.
**Context:** Avoid great sins; God knows you from the earth and the womb.
**Finding:** **The anti-self-certification rule.** A person may not *declare* himself pure. The
Qur'an forbids the verdict, not the act. Tazkiyah is a process one undergoes; "I am purified" is a
claim one may not make. This is the exact counter-verse to 4:49 and it sets the grammar for the
whole B-cluster.

# CLUSTER B — THE CLAIM AND THE GRANT
Who may purify, and who is refused.

## B1 — 4:49 (يزكون أنفسهم)
**Text:** أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِينَ يُزَكُّونَ أَنفُسَهُم ۚ بَلِ اللَّهُ يُزَكِّي مَن يَشَاءُ …
**Grammar:** human Form II active plural vs divine Form II active singular; `bal` corrects the first
with the second.
**Context:** Those who boast of purity; God purifies whom He wills; they are not wronged a thread.
**Finding:** The contrast is **claim vs grant**. The same verb, two subjects. Human self-purification
is a boast; divine purification is the reality. And the verse denies any injustice in the grant — the
distribution of purity is God's to make.

## B2 — 24:21 (ما زكى منكم من أحد)
**Text:** وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَتُهُ مَا زَكَىٰ مِنكُم مِّنْ أَحَدٍ أَبَدًا وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يُزَكِّي مَن يَشَاءُ
**Grammar:** Form I `zakā` negated with `mā … min aḥad … abadan` — the strongest negation; then
Form II divine active.
**Context:** Do not follow Satan's steps; he commands indecency and wrong.
**Finding:** **THE GRANT.** Even the root's simplest verb — *to be pure* — is impossible for anyone
without God's favour and mercy. Purity is not an achievement; it is a gift that the commanded act
receives. This verse alone forbids reading zakāh as a self-salvation mechanism.

## B3 — 2:174 (ولا يزكيهم)
**Text:** … وَمَا يَأْكُلُونَ فِي بُطُونِهِمْ إِلَّا النَّارَ … وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمْ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ
**Grammar:** negated Form II with divine subject.
**Context:** Those who conceal what God sent down and sell it for a small price.
**Finding:** **Withholding purification is the punishment.** The triple sentence — no speech, no
purification, painful torment — places the loss of tazkiyah among the worst outcomes. Note the
parallel to 4:10: concealing revelation is "eating fire into the belly." The anti-zakāh is not
merely stinginess; it is the sale of what should be given.

## B4 — 3:77 (ولا يزكيهم)
**Text:** … لَا خَلَاقَ لَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ وَلَا يُكَلِّمُهُمُ اللَّهُ وَلَا يَنظُرُ إِلَيْهِمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمْ …
**Grammar:** four negations in sequence.
**Context:** Those who sell God's covenant and their oaths for a small price.
**Finding:** The **four-fold negation**: no share, no speech, no look, no purification. The
"small price" (`thaman qalīl`) is the mirror-image of zakāh: they *sell* what should be *given*, and
they take a price instead of seeking the Face. Zakāh is giving-for-God; this is selling-for-price.
The honor-economy is exact.

# CLUSTER C — THE PURIFIER SENT
The Messenger as the agent of tazkiyah.

## C1 — 2:129 (ويزكيهم)
**Text:** رَبَّنَا وَابْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِكَ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ
**Grammar:** four imperfect verbs in Abraham's prayer: recite, teach the Book, teach wisdom, purify.
**Context:** Abraham and Ishmael at the House, praying for a messenger from among the descendants.
**Finding:** **Tazkiyah is the goal of revelation and the Messenger's commission.** It is not a
collection-office; it is a formation. The prayer places purification *after* teaching in this verse
and *before* teaching in 3:164 — the two orders are not a contradiction but a loop: recitation and
teaching feed purification, and purification makes teaching land.

## C2 — 2:151 (ويزكيكم)
**Text:** كَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا فِيكُمْ رَسُولًا مِّنكُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْكُمْ آيَاتِنَا وَيُزَكِّيكُمْ وَيُعَلِّمُكُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ
**Grammar:** second-person plural object — the audience is addressed directly.
**Context:** The changed qibla passage; the favour of a messenger "from among you."
**Finding:** Purification is **mediated by a human who shares your condition** (`minkum`). It is not
a distant decree. The messenger's nearness is part of the mechanism.

## C3 — 3:164 (ويزكيهم)
**Text:** لَقَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ بَعَثَ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًا مِّنْ أَنفُسِهِمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ
**Grammar:** `manna` — to confer a favour, to lay a debt of kindness.
**Context:** The favour of the messenger to the believers.
**Finding:** Purification is a **favour** (`mannah`), not a wage. The verse's order — recite, purify,
teach — makes tazkiyah the hinge between hearing and knowing. One is purified in order to learn.

## C4 — 62:2 (ويزكيهم)
**Text:** هُوَ الَّذِي بَعَثَ فِي الْأُمِّيِّينَ رَسُولًا مِّنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ وَإِن كَانُوا مِن قَبْلُ لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُّبِينٍ
**Grammar:** `in kānū min qablu la-fī ḍalāl mubīn` — the before/after contrast.
**Context:** The unlettered community before the messenger.
**Finding:** Tazkiyah is an **exodus from clear error**. The "before" is a state of being lost; the
"after" is purification and teaching. This is the same transfer-structure as the Ṣ-L-W study's
"bringing out of darkness into light" (33:43): the root names a movement from one state to another,
not a static label.

# CLUSTER D — THE FOUNDING FORMULA
Covenant, prophets, and the ṣalāh-zakāh pair.

## D1 — 98:5 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ حُنَفَاءَ وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَذَٰلِكَ دِينُ الْقَيِّمَةِ
**Grammar:** `mā … illā` — restriction: they were commanded *only* this; four coordinated acts.
**Context:** The clear proof has come; the People of the Book and the polytheists are addressed.
**Finding:** **THE MINIMAL RELIGION.** Zakāh is one of four constitutive acts of `dīn al-qayyimah`,
the upright religion. It is not an optional charity added to religion; it is religion's structure.
Note that `qayyimah` belongs to the standing-wordfield of the other study: the upright religion is
the *standing* religion, and zakāh is one of its load-bearing walls.

## D2 — 2:43 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَارْكَعُوا مَعَ الرَّاكِعِينَ
**Grammar:** three imperatives; the third adds a communal posture.
**Context:** The Children of Israel are commanded.
**Finding:** Zakāh is commanded **in the same breath as the standing**, to a whole community, with a
shared posture. The pair is not two separate duties that happen to be listed together; it is one
sentence with one subject: the community.

## D3 — 2:83 (الزكاة)
**Text:** … وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّيْتُم إِلَّا قَلِيلًا مِّنكُمْ …
**Grammar:** `thumma tawallaytum` — then you turned away.
**Context:** The Israelite covenant: no other god, excellence to parents, relatives, orphans, the
poor, good speech, establish, give.
**Finding:** Zakāh is embedded in a **social-ethical covenant**, and its breach is named by the verb
`tawallā` — the same turning-away that the Ṣ-L-W fire-verses use for the delivered (4:115, 92:16).
Turning from the giving is the same motion as turning from the standing.

## D4 — 5:12 (الزكاة)
**Text:** لَئِنْ أَقَمْتُمُ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَيْتُمُ الزَّكَاةَ وَآمَنتُم بِرُسُلِي وَعَزَّرْتُمُوهُمْ وَأَقْرَضْتُمُ اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا لَّأُكَفِّرَنَّ عَنكُمْ سَيِّئَاتِكُمْ
**Grammar:** five conditional clauses + oath + triple emphatic answer.
**Context:** The covenant with the Children of Israel and the twelve leaders.
**Finding:** Zakāh is **condition #2 of a five-condition covenant**, and the fifth condition is
"lend God a good loan." The economic axis runs through the whole covenant: give the due, support the
messengers, and lend to God. The promised return is the covering of sins and gardens — the increase
is transferred to the hereafter.

## D5 — 2:110 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ ۚ وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا لِأَنفُسِكُم مِّنْ خَيْرٍ تَجِدُوهُ عِندَ اللَّهِ
**Grammar:** `mā tuqaddimū li-anfusikum` — whatever you send ahead for yourselves.
**Context:** The command to the believers.
**Finding:** Zakāh is **SENT AHEAD**. The verb `qaddama` (send forward) makes the giver a forwarder:
he moves value from the present into the future, and finds it with God. It is an investment, not a
loss. The same verb recurs in 73:20 with the same promise.

## D6 — 2:177 (الزكاة)
**Text:** … وَآتَى الْمَالَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ ذَوِي الْقُرْبَىٰ وَالْيَتَامَىٰ وَالْمَسَاكِينَ وَابْنَ السَّبِيلِ وَالسَّائِلِينَ وَفِي الرِّقَابِ وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ …
**Grammar:** a long character-list; zakāh is one item after the list of wealth-givings.
**Context:** The definition of `birr` (righteousness).
**Finding:** Zakāh appears **after** the spontaneous givings (relatives, orphans, the poor, the
wayfarer, the askers, slaves). It is therefore a *second layer*: the open hand of love, then the
standing obligation of the community. And the giver gave "despite love of it" (`ʿalā ḥubbihi`) —
the inner cost is named.

## D7 — 2:277 (الزكاة)
**Text:** إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ لَهُمْ أَجْرُهُمْ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ وَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ
**Grammar:** four relative clauses + triple promise: reward, no fear, no grief.
**Context:** Immediately after the ribā verses (2:275-276).
**Finding:** **Zakāh is the anti-ribā act.** The givers have no fear and no grief — the exact
opposite of the anxious accumulator. And 2:276, one verse earlier, says God "makes the charities
grow" (`yurbī al-ṣadaqāt`). The economic contrast is deliberate: ribā is erased, giving is grown.

## D8 — 7:156 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَرَحْمَتِي وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ ۚ فَسَأَكْتُبُهَا لِلَّذِينَ يَتَّقُونَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ بِآيَاتِنَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
**Grammar:** `fa-sa-aktubuhā` — I will write it (the mercy) for…
**Context:** Musa's plea; God's answer.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **condition for mercy being written**. The giver is inside the circle of
mercy; the withholder is outside it. Note the order: guard, give, believe — the ethical act sits
between fear and faith.

## D9 — 19:31 (والزكاة)
**Text:** وَجَعَلَنِي مُبَارَكًا أَيْنَ مَا كُنتُ وَأَوْصَانِي بِالصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ مَا دُمْتُ حَيًّا
**Grammar:** `awṣānī` — enjoined me; `mā dumtu ḥayyā` — as long as I live.
**Context:** Jesus speaks in the cradle.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **prophetic enjoinder** (`waṣiyyah`), lifelong, tied to `barakah`
(blessedness). Barakah is increase — the root's growth sense appears in the same sentence. And there
is no state, treasury, or quota in the verse: the command is personal and lifelong.

## D10 — 19:55 (والزكاة)
**Text:** وَكَانَ يَأْمُرُ أَهْلَهُ بِالصَّلَاةِ وَالزَّكَاةِ وَكَانَ عِندَ رَبِّهِ مَرْضِيًّا
**Grammar:** `kāna yaʾmuru` — habitual command; `ahlahu` — his household.
**Context:** Ishmael, true to his promise.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **household command**, transmitted like the standing. The family is the first
unit of the institution. And the reward is `marḍiyyan` — pleased with him: the giving produces
divine pleasure, not merely a receipt.

## D11 — 21:73 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَجَعَلْنَاهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْهِمْ فِعْلَ الْخَيْرَاتِ وَإِقَامَ الصَّلَاةِ وَإِيتَاءَ الزَّكَاةِ
**Grammar:** `awḥaynā ilayhim` — We inspired them; three maṣdars: doing good, establishing, giving.
**Context:** The leaders made by God, guiding by His command.
**Finding:** **Ītāʾ al-zakāh is INSPIRED.** The giving is a revelation-shaped act and a mark of
leadership. The leaders are defined by what they *give*, not by what they *collect*. This single
verse dismantles every reading of zakāh as a revenue instrument.

## D12 — 22:78 (الزكاة)
**Text:** … وَمَا جَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ مِنْ حَرَجٍ … فَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِاللَّهِ
**Grammar:** `min ḥaraj` — any constriction; three final imperatives.
**Context:** Striving for God; the milla of Abraham; named Muslims.
**Finding:** Zakāh belongs to the **eased religion**. It cannot be a crushing burden, because the
same verse denies any constriction in the religion. Establish, give, hold fast — the three acts end
in clinging to God, not in a ledger.

## D13 — 4:162 (الزكاة)
**Text:** … وَالْمُقِيمِينَ الصَّلَاةَ وَالْمُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ … أُولَٰئِكَ سَنُؤْتِيهِمْ أَجْرًا عَظِيمًا
**Grammar:** accusative `al-muqīmīna` (praise-case) and nominative `al-muʾtūna` — two participial
classes.
**Context:** The firmly-rooted in knowledge and the believers.
**Finding:** The givers are a **distinct category** alongside the firmly-rooted in knowledge. Zakāh
is an act of the knowing. And the promise is `ajran ʿaẓīmā` — a great wage, the language of a
return on what was sent ahead.

## D14 — 5:55 (الزكاة)
**Text:** إِنَّمَا وَلِيُّكُمُ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا الَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَهُمْ رَاكِعُونَ
**Grammar:** `innamā` restriction + two participles + circumstantial `rākiʿūn`.
**Context:** The definition of God's allies.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **mark of God's party**. The giver is an ally of God — the highest
political title in the Qur'an is given to those who give. And "while bowing" fuses the two acts: the
same movement that bows gives.

# CLUSTER E — THE INSTITUTION
Community, power, economy, and the increase.

## E1 — 9:5 (الزكاة)
**Text:** فَإِن تَابُوا وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ فَخَلُّوا سَبِيلَهُمْ
**Grammar:** three conditions + imperative release.
**Context:** After the sacred months, the command concerning the hostile polytheists.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **peace-condition** and the proof of repentance. The verse is about ending
hostility: the giving is the sign of a changed allegiance. A person who gives the due has re-entered
the common order and his way is released.

## E2 — 9:11 (الزكاة)
**Text:** فَإِن تَابُوا وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ فَإِخْوَانُكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ
**Grammar:** `fa-` + nominal sentence: then they are your brothers.
**Context:** The same three conditions, one verse later.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **brotherhood-condition**. Withholding it keeps one outside the bond. This
is the strongest social claim the word makes: the giving is not a private virtue but the price of
belonging.

## E3 — 9:18 (الزكاة)
**Text:** إِنَّمَا يَعْمُرُ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ مَن آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَى الزَّكَاةَ وَلَمْ يَخْشَ إِلَّا اللَّهَ
**Grammar:** `innamā` restriction; four conditions.
**Context:** Who truly maintains God's houses.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **condition for building**. The one who gives is the one who maintains. The
verse also names the fear-test: only God is feared, so wealth is not hoarded out of fear of poverty.

## E4 — 9:71 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ يَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَيُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ
**Grammar:** five imperfect plurals; both genders.
**Context:** The mutual alliance of believing men and women.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **economic nerve of the believing community** — men and women alike. The
pair (enjoining good + giving) defines the public body. A community that does not give is not the
community the verse describes.

## E5 — 9:103 (الزكاة) — THE MECHANISM
**Text:** خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ صَلَاتَكَ سَكَنٌ لَّهُمْ
**Grammar:** imperative `khudh`; `min amwālihim` (from their wealth, partitive); two verbs with one
instrument `bihā`.
**Context:** The command to take charity from the believers, and the promise of tranquility.
**Finding:** **THE DEFINITION.** The Qur'an itself glosses tazkiyah as the effect of taking a portion
of wealth: it **cleanses** them and **grows** them. `Ṭahāra` removes; `zakāh` increases. One act,
two effects. The partitive `min` is decisive: it is *from* the wealth, not the wealth itself. And the
Verse links the giving to the Prophet's standing (`ṣalātika sakan`) — the two roots of this project
touch again.

## E6 — 22:41 (الزكاة)
**Text:** الَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَمَرُوا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ
**Grammar:** `in makkannāhum` — if We establish them; four aorist acts.
**Context:** The believers who are given power in the land.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **first act of the empowered**. The test of power is whether it gives, not
whether it collects. The order — establish, give, enjoin, forbid — puts the transfer before the
public moral command.

## E7 — 23:4 (الزكاة) — THE ACT
**Text:** وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِلزَّكَاةِ فَاعِلُونَ
**Grammar:** `li-l-zakāti fāʿilūn` — active participle, "doers/makers of the zakāh."
**Context:** The believers' portrait (23:1-11): humility, guarding, chastity, the zakāh, trusts,
covenants, standings.
**Finding:** **THE STRONGEST "ACT, NOT TAX" PROOF.** Elsewhere the verb is `ātā` (give); here the
Qur'an calls the zakāh a **work with doers**. A tax is paid; a zakāh is *done*. And it sits between
guarding chastity and guarding trusts — a character-item, not a line-item. This verse alone makes the
reduction of zakāh to a percentage impossible.

## E8 — 24:37 (الزكاة)
**Text:** رِجَالٌ لَّا تُلْهِيهِمْ تِجَارَةٌ وَلَا بَيْعٌ عَن ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَإِقَامِ الصَّلَاةِ وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ
**Grammar:** `lā tulhīhim` — neither trade nor sale diverts them.
**Context:** Men in houses where God's Name is mentioned.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **economic test**. It is precisely trade and sale that could distract from
it, so the verse names them. The giver's wealth is active in the market, and the zakāh is the point
where the market bows. The triad — remembrance, establishing, giving — is the full public life.

## E9 — 24:56 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُرْحَمُونَ
**Grammar:** three imperatives + `laʿalla` (hope/purpose).
**Context:** The promise of succession and the command to the believers.
**Finding:** Zakāh is a **condition for mercy**, paired with obedience to the Messenger. The giving
is not a private transaction with God; it is part of the public obedience that earns mercy.

## E10 — 27:3 (الزكاة)
**Text:** الَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَهُم بِالْآخِرَةِ هُم يوقِنونَ
**Grammar:** two participles + emphatic `hum` + `yūqinūn`.
**Context:** The guidance and good news for the believers.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the act of the **Hereafter-certain**. The one who is sure of the return gives
now. The participle `muʾtūn` names a standing practice, not a one-time payment.

## E11 — 30:39 (زكاة) — THE PIVOT
**Text:** وَمَا آتَيْتُم مِّن رِّبًا لِّيَرْبُوَ فِي أَمْوَالِ النَّاسِ فَلَا يَرْبُو عِندَ اللَّهِ ۖ وَمَا آتَيْتُم مِّن زَكَاةٍ تُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُضْعِفُونَ
**Grammar:** two parallel `wa-mā ātaytum` clauses; `r-b-w` in the first, `z-k-w` in the second;
`al-muḍʿifūn` active participle.
**Context:** The chapter on the Romans' defeat and the economics of giving.
**Finding:** **THE PIVOT OF THE WHOLE STUDY.** The Qur'an uses the increase-verb (`r-b-w`) for ribā
and denies it increase with God; it uses `zakāh` for the giving that God *does* multiply, and names
its givers **al-muḍʿifūn — the multipliers**. The root's growth sense is not imported from a lexicon;
the Qur'an states it. Ribā claims increase and has none; zakāh is the true increase. Note also the
intention clause `turīdūna wajha Allāh` — the same `wajh` as 92:20.

## E12 — 31:4 (الزكاة)
**Text:** الَّذِينَ يُقِيمُونَ الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَهُم بِالْآخِرَةِ هُم يوقِنونَ
**Grammar:** identical to 27:3.
**Context:** The guided and the successful.
**Finding:** **The formula is stable across suras.** Word-for-word identity with 27:3 proves the
ṣalāh-zakāh-certainty triad is a fixed Qur'anic formula, not a local instruction. A fixed formula
means a fixed institution.

## E13 — 33:33 (الزكاة)
**Text:** وَأَقِمْنَ الصَّلَاةَ وَآتِينَ الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِعْنَ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ … إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيُذْهِبَ عَنكُمُ الرِّجْسَ أَهْلَ الْبَيْتِ وَيُطَهِّرَكُمْ تَطْهِيرًا
**Grammar:** feminine imperatives; `li-yudhhiba` + `yuṭahhira` — remove and purify.
**Context:** The Prophet's household.
**Finding:** The verse ends with **ṭahāra** — the same companion-root as 9:103. Remove the impurity,
then purify. Zakāh and purification are one movement: the giving takes out what corrupts and grows
what remains. The command is addressed to women as fully as to men.

## E14 — 41:7 (الزكاة)
**Text:** الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَهُم بِالْآخِرَةِ هُم كَافِرُونَ
**Grammar:** negated giving coordinated with active disbelief.
**Context:** The warning to those who turn away.
**Finding:** **THE NEGATIVE DEFINITION.** Withholding zakāh is grammatically coordinated with
disbelief in the Hereafter. The giver believes in the return; the withholder denies it. This is the
sharpest statement of what the word claims: it is not a charitable extra but a test of whether one
believes anything comes back.

## E15 — 58:13 (الزكاة)
**Text:** فَإِذ لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا وَتَابَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْكُمْ فَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ
**Grammar:** `fa-idh lam tafʿalū wa-tāba Allāhu ʿalaykum` — failure, then divine turning.
**Context:** The charity before private consultation; they did not do it; God turned in forgiveness.
**Finding:** Zakāh follows **forgiveness**. The failed giver is not excluded; he is re-commanded.
Grace precedes the act, and the act is the shape grace takes afterwards.

## E16 — 73:20 (الزكاة)
**Text:** فَاقْرَءُوا مَا تَيَسَّرَ مِنَ الْقُرْآنِ … وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَقْرِضُوا اللَّهَ قَرْضًا حَسَنًا
**Grammar:** `mā tayassara` — what is easy; `aqriḍū Allāh` — lend to God.
**Context:** The night-standing concession; the sick, the travellers, the fighters.
**Finding:** The **eased arrangement**. Zakāh is commanded in the same breath as the concession, so
the obligation is calibrated to capacity. And the "loan" language makes the giver a creditor of God:
the increase is guaranteed by the borrower.

## E17 — 4:77 (الزكاة)
**Text:** … وَأَقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتُوا الزَّكَاةَ ۚ فَلَمَّا كُتِبَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْقِتَالُ … يَخْشَوْنَ النَّاسَ كَخَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ
**Grammar:** command, then `fa-lammā` — the reversal when fighting was prescribed.
**Context:** Those told to restrain their hands, establish, and give; then they feared people.
**Finding:** Zakāh is the **peace-act and the courage-training**. The same people who were commanded
to give feared the cost of fighting. Giving loosens the grip on wealth before life is asked for. The
verse places the purse and the sword on one axis: whoever cannot release a portion cannot release
himself.

# FIRST-PASS COMPLETE — 56/56

The root Z-K-W names **growth-through-purity**: the removal of what corrupts so that what remains
can increase. The Qur'an states the growth explicitly (30:39 `al-muḍʿifūn`), states the purity
explicitly (9:103 `tuṭahhiruhum wa tuzakkīhim`), and states the instrument explicitly (92:18: giving
wealth *is* self-purification). The noun `zakāh` is the institutional form of that act; the verb
`zakkā` names the act and its effect; the elative `azkā` names the grade.
